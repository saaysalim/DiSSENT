## UA-PD-1 Personal data
SAMPLE summary, not legal text. In Ukraine, sharing personal data about another person, such as a phone number, private email address, date of birth, passport or national ID number, needs a clear reason and should be limited to what is necessary. Sharing it without the person's knowledge can put them at risk.

## UA-SRC-1 Protection of sources
SAMPLE summary, not legal text. In Ukraine, journalists are expected to protect confidential sources. Naming a source, or giving details such as a unique job, family relation or workplace that make a source identifiable, can expose the source to harm and should be avoided.

## UA-LOC-1 Location and movement
SAMPLE summary, not legal text. In Ukraine, an exact address, GPS coordinates, the location of a shelter or safe house, or the time and place of a planned border crossing can endanger people if the message is seen by others.

## UA-FIN-1 Financial information
SAMPLE summary, not legal text. In Ukraine, bank account numbers (IBAN), payment card numbers, crypto wallet addresses and payment codes are sensitive financial data. They can be used for fraud or to trace people.

## UA-SEC-1 Account security
SAMPLE summary, not legal text. In Ukraine, passwords, login details and links that ask people to log in on another website must not be shared in messages. Shared or reused passwords let others take over accounts.

## UA-ATT-1 Attachments
SAMPLE summary, not legal text. In Ukraine, files such as photos and documents can contain faces, places and hidden details. Their content cannot be checked automatically, so a person should look at them first.
